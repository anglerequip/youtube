# Reference study: 3D skit ad, angles, dialogue, focus and clipping-free staging

Reference: TikTok by @one.bold.chemist, video 7704961984969657631 ("too many people on that horse"),
shared by Caesar 2026-11-26. 57.4 s, 960x720 source, about 1.7 M plays, 27.4 k likes (1.6 %), 299 comments
(public counts read 2026-11-26).

Method: frames sampled every 0.2 s in the browser, cuts found by frame difference, then 2 frames per shot
inspected (start and end) plus the hand-off moments up close. Dialogue from the video's own auto caption
track (WebVTT, timings below). Audio itself was not listened to, so music and SFX notes are not included.

**Study only.** We copy techniques, never the creator's characters, product, jokes, script, set or shots.
Our host and world stay original (see CLAUDE.md sections 11-13 and the AnglerMind project instructions).

---

## 1. What the video is

A product ad disguised as a two-character skit. A fan (blocky box-head character) asks the hero (lab-coat
chemist, goggles, gloves) to sign a perfume bottle; the hero spots it is a counterfeit, smashes it, shows
the real product and the real price, gifts it, then delivers the call to action. Setting: one space-station
window with Earth and a low, flaring sun behind. One set, two characters, three props (bottle, pen, box).

The ad works because the product is the *plot device*, not a slide at the end: "your copy is fake" is the
conflict, "the real one is cheaper and comes with more" is the twist, and the link is the resolution.

## 3. Dialogue and structure

Timeline of beats (caption track timings):

- **0.9 s Hook:** the fan's first line names the hero and asks for something. A question in the first second.
- **12 s Conflict:** "that's not ours". The product enters as a problem, not an advert.
- **13-15 s Comic turn:** a specific, visual joke about the fake's label. The joke is about something on screen.
- **20-30 s Proof:** real vs fake, the fan's price, the hero's disbelief ("are you serious?").
- **30-41 s Offer:** real price, what's in the box, extra freebies. Concrete numbers, said once.
- **41 s Emotional low:** the fan's wordless sad reaction, played for comedy.
- **43-50 s Generosity:** hero signs it and gifts the box. Makes the hero likeable just before the ask.
- **50-55 s CTA:** "don't buy from weird sites, use the link". The CTA is phrased as advice that follows from the plot.
- **55-57 s Button:** fan's short closing line. Ends on a character, not a logo.

Writing traits worth copying:
- Lines are short and conversational with slang and character voice; the hero is dry, the fan is over-eager.
  The contrast does the comedy.
- Each speaker usually gets one line, then the camera goes to the listener for a reaction.
- Numbers are spoken plainly and only the ones that matter (their price vs our price).
- Captions are the full spoken line in 1-2 lines, white with shadow, lower-middle of frame, and include
  one written gag ("sad noise*"). (Different from our 1-3 word caption rule; noted, not adopted.)

## 4. Focus, light and depth

- **Shallow depth of field on every close shot**, focus always on the speaker or the hero prop. Whatever is
  near the lens (the other character's shoulder, a hand, the bottle) is heavily blurred.
- **Three depth layers almost everywhere:** soft foreground character or prop, sharp subject, bright
  background (Earth + sun). This is exactly CLAUDE.md 13 rule 3.
- **Strong backlight:** low sun behind the hero gives a rim light and bloom on hair and props; faces get a
  warm soft key. The flare hides the background's simplicity.
- **Product shots** are held up to the lens, sharp, label facing camera, background blown out by the sun.
- Reaction close-ups of the fan are very tight (eyes and mouth fill the frame), which makes the simple face
  read big on a phone.

## 5. Why nothing clips (the main thing Caesar noticed)

The characters and props never visibly pass through each other. That is achieved by staging and cutting,
not by a perfect physics rig:

1. **Cut around contact.** The moment two models meet is almost never on screen. The gift box hand-off
   happens between shots (fan has it in shot 19, never shown changing hands). The signature appears already
   written when we cut back (shot 16 to 17). The smash happens in a wide where the bottle is tiny.
2. **Split grip on shared props.** In the one on-screen hand-off (shot 4), the fan holds the bottle's base
   and the hero's glove takes the top half. Two hands, two separate zones of the prop, never overlapping.
3. **Separation along the lens axis, not side by side.** Two-shots stack the characters in depth (one near
   the lens, one behind). On screen they overlap, but that is occlusion. In 3D they are a metre or more
   apart, so they cannot intersect. A longer lens makes them look close.
4. **Contact actions go in inserts.** Signing, holding the box open, presenting the bottle are tight inserts
   where only the hands and prop are in frame, posed by hand for that one camera. The rest of the body is
   out of focus behind.
5. **Reaching hands enter from the frame edge.** Shot 10: the hero's hand and bottle come in from the side
   of a close-up, stopping short of the face. You never see the arm, so no arm-through-body risk.
6. **Blurred foreground silhouettes.** The near character in over-the-shoulders is dark and defocused, so
   any hair, edge or overlap problem is invisible.
7. **Clip-resistant character design.** Gloves are chunky mitts (no thin fingers wrapping props), the fan
   has a box head with no neck or collar, the hero's coat hangs straight. Gestures go outward from the body
   (arm thrown wide in shot 7), not across it, so sleeves don't enter the torso.
8. **Few moving parts per shot.** Most shots animate the head and one arm only. Less motion, fewer chances
   to intersect.

## 6. What we take for AnglerMind (and the existing pipeline)

Techniques, translated to our renderer (`renderer/look.js` DOF, `shot.focus`, `shot.dof`):

- **Shot grammar:** one wide establishing shot per set, then close-ups, inserts and over-the-shoulders.
  New angle per line, short reaction shots (0.8-1.5 s) of the listener.
- **Three-layer frames with real DOF:** put the other character's shoulder or a prop near the lens and blur it
  (`shot.dof` 1.5 on close-ups), focus on the speaker with `shot.focus`.
- **No-contact staging rule (proposed for our scenes):**
  - Never animate two models touching in one continuous shot. Cut before contact and after it.
  - A shared prop (rod, lure, phone showing the app) is held at separate grip zones, or is passed off screen.
  - Two-shots stack characters in depth, at least about 1 m apart in z.
  - Hand + prop actions (tying a knot, picking a lure, tapping the app) are tight inserts posed for that
    camera, body out of focus.
  - A self-check could measure the minimum distance between character meshes and props per frame and flag
    intersections before render (a cheap bounding-volume test fits our existing `self_checks()` pattern).
- **Product as plot:** for AnglerMind, the app must be the thing that resolves a fishing problem the host or a
  second character has (wrong lure colour, wrong spot, rod and reel that don't balance), not a closing card.
  The CTA then reads as advice that follows from the story.
- **Character contrast:** a dry, knowing host plus an over-eager second character gives comedy without jokes
  that need setup. Our host (Finn) and any second character must be our own designs.
- **Backlight and flare** to make a simple set look rich: a low sun behind the host, rim light, bloom on props.

Not adopted (conflicts with our rules or is the creator's identity): full-sentence captions (we keep 1-3
words), the space-station set, the box-head fan, the chemist look, the counterfeit-perfume story, the
label joke and any of their wording.
